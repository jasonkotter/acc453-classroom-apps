# Instructor guide: Barter Market

**Goal.** On day 1, students feel the double coincidence of wants, then watch money remove it.
About 15 minutes.

## Setup

1. Open `barter-market/?view=instructor` on the laptop, create a session, and put the projector
   screen (link on the instructor page) on the projector. It shows the code and a QR code.
2. Students join on their phones. This is the first class, so allow 3 minutes for joining; it
   doubles as practice for every later game.
3. Rehearse first with `?demo=1` (add simulated students), or with `_shared/bots.html` against
   a live session.

## Run

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 3 min | Join. Read the opening script. |
| 4 min | **Barter.** Show round → Open trading. Students stand up, walk, and trade. Close trading when the timer reaches 0:00. Reveal. |
| 4 min | **Money.** Show next round (already selected) → Open trading → close at 0:00 → Reveal. |
| 4 min | Debrief with the comparison on the screen (Begin debrief shows the questions). |

## Opening script

"Everyone has one good and wants a different one. Look at your phone: it shows your trading tag,
what you have, and what you want. Stand up, walk around, and talk: call out what you have and
what you want. When you find a trade, enter your partner's tag and the goods and send the offer;
they accept on their phone. Once you have what you want, you're done. You have three minutes."
For the money round: "Same game, new goods, and everyone also has one token. Anyone will take a
token for a good."

## What to expect

- Barter: a minority finish, mostly in the first minute (the lucky double coincidences), then the
  line goes flat. A few students may start accepting goods they do not want; ask them why.
- Money: most finish, usually in two trades each (sell what you have, buy what you want).
- Students who arrive late play from the next round.

## Debrief questions

**On screen.** **Begin debrief** puts three things on the projector and on every phone: an *explain* prompt that quotes this round's numbers, a *transfer* question, and a private exit check (one question on the phone; the reasoning and a common mistake appear after the student answers). Give pairs about two minutes for the first two, then a minute for the exit check. Exit-check answers are not recorded, graded, or timed. When you show the next round, the projector also lists what changed from the last round and what stayed fixed, and asks for a prediction before anyone plays. The questions below go deeper.

- Why did barter stall? What did you need to find?
- Did anyone take a good they did not want? Which good would you have taken, and why?
- What did the token do? Would it have worked if no one trusted it?
- Which functions of money did you just see? (Medium of exchange first; the others are 01.2.)

## Fallback without the app

Index cards: "You have: Fish. You want: Salt." (make each good held and wanted equally often).
Three minutes of barter, then hand out one paper token per student and repeat with new cards.
Count hands for "got what you wanted" after each round and write the two numbers on the board.
