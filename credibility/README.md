# Can the Central Bank Be Believed?

Apr 6, Ch. 12 and 14 (replaces part of the independence discussion; connects to Turkey,
2021–23). Runs on the shared toolkit. About 15–20 minutes. Design: `../../APP_BUILD_PLAN.md`,
"Shortlist of new shared games".

## Rules

- Each round a **policy team** of 3 is picked at random (new team every round); everyone else is
  the **public**, setting wages and prices.
- **4 years**, two steps each. Step 1: each member of the public forecasts this year's
  inflation (0–15%, half points). Step 2: the team chooses this year's inflation (the median of
  its members' votes) and may announce a plan for next year.
- **Unemployment = 5% − (inflation − the public's average forecast).** Only a surprise moves it.
- **Public score:** total points its forecasts missed by (lower is better).
- **Team score:** (inflation − 2)² + 2 × (unemployment − 3)² each year, summed (lower is
  better). The team wants 2% inflation and unemployment *below* its natural rate, which is the
  temptation. Its phone shows a calculator: for each inflation choice, the unemployment and score
  it would get given this year's forecasts.
- Defaults: no forecasts = last year's inflation; no team answer = 2%.

The arithmetic: given a forecast πᵉ, the team's best inflation is (2 + 2(2 + πᵉ)) / 3, so with
πᵉ = 2 it chooses about 3.3%. If the public sees this coming, inflation settles at **6%** with
unemployment back at 5%: no jobs gained, only inflation (Barro–Gordon).

## Treatments (presets)

1. **Discretion.** The team chooses each year after seeing forecasts; announcements are words.
2. **Commitment.** A new team. Each year's announced plan *becomes* next year's inflation (year
   1: the 2% target), so the team cannot surprise anyone.
3. **A conservative central banker.** A new team, choosing each year again, but scored on
   inflation only (Rogoff's conservative banker; a price-stability mandate).

## Run

Each round: Show round → Open year 1 forecasts → Close year 1 forecasts → End year 1 → … → End
the last year → Reveal. The projector shows announced, forecast, and actual inflation and
unemployment by year, with a round comparison.

**Demo (40 simulated students, 2026-11-24):** discretion, inflation 3.5% → 4.5% → 4.5% → 5%
(average 4.4%) while unemployment drifted back from 3.4% toward 4.3% (average 4.1%), always
announced at 2%; commitment, 2.0% inflation and 5.0% unemployment every year; conservative
banker, 2.0% inflation, 5.4% unemployment. Simulated forecasters mostly extrapolate last year's
inflation; real students may trust longer or punish faster.

**Debrief.** Why did the class stop believing the announcements? Did surprise inflation buy
lasting jobs? What made the commitment round credible, and what real institutions play that role
(independence, mandates, inflation targets, the cost to a central banker's reputation)? Turkey
2021–23: what happens to expectations when the central bank is seen as taking orders? The game
illustrates a mechanism; it does not prove that independence alone explains any country's
inflation.

**Fallback.** Forecasts on slips; three volunteers choose inflation at the front with the
calculator table on the board.
