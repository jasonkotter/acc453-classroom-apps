# Instructor guide: Hidden Effort

**Class:** Tuesday, February 9, after Lemons Market. **Book:** Ch. 6 (hidden action; screening
and monitoring). **Time:** about 10–15 minutes. The PPP or LendingClub case may take this slot
instead.

## Goal

Lemons Market was about what a seller knows; this is about what a borrower does after getting
the money. A lender who cannot see effort may raise the interest rate to cover the risk, but a
higher rate leaves the borrower less to gain from working, so it can cause the very slacking it
was meant to price. Students should leave able to explain moral hazard, why lenders ration credit
instead of simply charging more, and what monitoring and collateral each fix.

## How a round works

1. **Lenders and borrowers**, paired at random. With an odd number, one student watches.
2. **The loan.** A borrower needs $100 for a project that pays $160 if it succeeds and nothing if
   it fails. A borrower repays only if the project succeeds.
3. **The lender sets the interest rate** (0–60%). Lenders also guess what share of borrowers will
   slack, and borrowers guess the average rate.
4. **The borrower chooses, hidden from the lender:** work (costs the borrower $10; succeeds 90% of
   the time), slack (free; succeeds 50%), or decline. No answer from a borrower means decline.

**The tipping point:** working beats slacking only at rates up to **35%**. At 35% a lender
expects to gain $21.50 if the borrower works; at 36% with a borrower who slacks, it expects to
lose $32.

**Treatments.** Monitoring lets a lender pay $5 to watch the borrower: a borrower caught slacking
has the loan called back, so a monitored borrower should work at rates up to 48%. Collateral has
each borrower pledge $30, kept by the lender on a failure, and working then beats slacking at
every allowed rate.

## Rounds and timing

| Time | Round | What to say before opening |
|---|---|---|
| 2 min | Join and rules | Read the opening script. |
| 3 min | 1. Hidden effort | Nothing more. |
| 3 min | 2. Hidden effort, new partners (optional) | "Lenders, look at the chart: which rates got effort?" |
| 3 min | 3. Lenders can monitor | "Lenders can now pay $5 to watch their borrower. A borrower caught slacking has the loan called." |
| 3 min | 4. Borrowers post collateral | "No monitoring. Each borrower pledges $30, and the lender keeps it if the project fails." |
| 3 min | Debrief | |

Each round: **Open: lenders set terms**, **End terms; send them to borrowers**, **Close the
round**, **Reveal**. Give each step about 30 seconds. If time is short, skip round 2 or the
collateral round and describe it.

## Setup

Create the session, open the projector screen on the classroom display, and wait for the joined
count to match the room.

## Opening script

"Half of you are lenders and half are borrowers. Lenders, you lend $100 and set the interest
rate. Borrowers, you'll see the rate and then decide whether to work hard on your project or
take it easy. Working costs you $10 but almost always pays off. Taking it easy is free but fails
half the time, and if the project fails, you don't repay. Lenders never see which you chose."

## Reading the results screen

The bar chart stacks borrowers' choices by the rate their lender charged and marks where
slacking starts to pay. The lenders' guess of the slack share sits next to the actual share,
which is the quickest way to ask whether lenders saw it coming.

## What to expect

This has not been run with students. With 40 simulated students, who chose their best option 80%
of the time, 20% and then 30% of borrowers slacked under hidden effort, 5% with monitoring, and
none with collateral; the value created went from 52–68% of the maximum under hidden effort to
83% with monitoring and 100% with collateral. The
live rehearsal with 12 bots produced the same pattern. Students may work out of loyalty to a
classmate even when slacking pays, which is worth pointing out: lenders cannot count on that
with strangers.

## Debrief questions

**On screen.** **Begin debrief** puts three things on the projector and on every phone: an *explain* prompt that quotes this round's numbers, a *transfer* question, and a private exit check (one question on the phone; the reasoning and a common mistake appear after the student answers). Give pairs about two minutes for the first two, then a minute for the exit check. Exit-check answers are not recorded, graded, or timed. When you show the next round, the projector also lists what changed from the last round and what stayed fixed, and asks for a prediction before anyone plays. The questions below go deeper.

- At what rate did borrowers start slacking? Did the lenders' guesses anticipate it?
- Why can't a lender solve the problem by charging more?
- What did monitoring buy, and why is it cheaper for one bank to monitor than for many small
  lenders (06.3)?
- How is collateral different from monitoring? Who bears the cost of each?
- Where else does a lender limit what it cannot see? (Covenants, down payments, deductibles.)

## Fallback without the app

Pair students on paper. The lender writes a rate on a slip and passes it over; the borrower
circles work or slack; roll a ten-sided die (or use a random number) for success: 1–9 for work,
1–5 for slack.
