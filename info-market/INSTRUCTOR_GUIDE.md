# Instructor guide: Information Market

**Class:** Tuesday, March 16, after 10.1 (credit spreads). **Book:** Ch. 10. **Time:** about
15–20 minutes. Mar 16 is the heaviest day of the term, so this is optional: it replaces Recession
Call or part of the March 2020 corporate-bond case, never both.

## Goal

Each student knows a little about whether a borrower will repay, and nobody knows the answer.
A single market price can still end up close to what all the clues together imply. Students
should leave able to explain how a bond price aggregates information, whose information reaches
the price and whose does not, and why a price can move on news without anyone learning anything
new about the borrower.

## How a round works

1. **The borrower** will repay (a claim pays $100) or default (pays $0), equally likely.
2. **Every student** starts with $100 cash and one claim, and may get a private clue ("sales rose"
   or "sales fell") that is right a set share of the time.
3. **Two markets.** In each, a student enters a probability that the borrower repays and one
   order: buy a claim at up to a price, sell one at no less than a price, or sit out.
4. **One clearing price** per market: the price at which the most claims trade. Everyone trades
   at that price. If nothing trades, the screen shows the best bid and best ask.
5. **Between the markets** a rating change (upgrade or downgrade) is announced; it is right 70%
   of the time.
6. **At the end** the claims pay off. The score is cash plus claims times the payoff, compared
   with not trading.

The outcome is kept off students' phones: it is drawn from the instructor's private session
secret, so run the round from the browser that created the session.

## Rounds and timing

| Time | Round | What to say before opening |
|---|---|---|
| 2 min | Join and rules | Read the opening script. |
| 5 min | 1. Everyone gets a weak clue | "Your clue is right about two times in three." |
| 4 min | 2. A few insiders | "A new borrower. One in five of you has a clue, and it's right 90% of the time." |
| 4 min | 3. No clues | Optional. "A new borrower, and nobody has a clue. Only the rating change." |
| 4 min | Debrief | |

Each round: **Open market 1**, **Clear market 1; announce the news**, **Close market 2**, then
**Reveal**.

## Setup

Create the session from the same browser you will run it on, open the projector screen on the
classroom display, and wait for the joined count to match the room.

## Opening script

"A company borrowed money, and it will either pay back $100 or nothing. Each of you holds one claim
on that $100 and has $100 in cash. Some of you got a clue about how the company's sales went. You
can buy, sell, or sit out, and everyone trades at one price. Then a rating agency will say
something, and you'll trade again."

## Reading the results screen

The reveal shows the outcome, how many clues pointed each way, what all the clues together
implied, both market prices, and a chart of the prices against the pooled clues and the payoff.
The comparison to read aloud is the price against what the pooled clues implied.

## What to expect

This has not been run with students. With 40 simulated traders and weak clues, market 1 cleared at
$53 against pooled clues that implied about $98, and buyers bid up to $72 after an upgrade; the
borrower repaid. With insiders, the price fell from $46 toward $34 and the borrower defaulted.
With no clues, a wrong downgrade pushed asks to $38 on a borrower that repaid. Simulated traders
often agreed so completely after the news that market 2 had no trades; real students will
disagree more. If nobody trades, ask why.

## Debrief questions

**On screen.** **Begin debrief** puts three things on the projector and on every phone: an *explain* prompt that quotes this round's numbers, a *transfer* question, and a private exit check (one question on the phone; the reasoning and a common mistake appear after the student answers). Give pairs about two minutes for the first two, then a minute for the exit check. Exit-check answers are not recorded, graded, or timed. When you show the next round, the projector also lists what changed from the last round and what stayed fixed, and asks for a prediction before anyone plays. The questions below go deeper.

- Nobody in the room knew the answer. How close did the price get, and how?
- Whose information reached the price, and who sat out? Did the insiders trade?
- Did the rating change add information, or just move everyone the same way?
- What besides default risk moves a bond price? (Liquidity, an expected buyer.)
- March 2020: the Fed's announcement promised a future buyer. How much of the price move was
  that, and how much was news about the borrowers? Avoid attributing all of it to the
  announcement.

## Fallback without the app

Index cards with clues; students write buy or sell slips with a price; find the clearing price on
the board by sorting the slips.
