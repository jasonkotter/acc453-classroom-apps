# Bond Showdown

Feb 25, after 08.4–08.6. Runs on the shared toolkit. Two rounds.

## Bonds

Annual coupons, both at face value today: a **2-year at 4.0%** and a **10-year at 4.5%**. A rate
move shifts both yields by the same amount and reprices them at once (no coupon accrues, so the
lesson is price–yield inversion and duration, not holding-period returns). One point: 2-year
−1.86% / +1.91%, 10-year −7.54% / +8.32%. Two points up: 2-year −3.67%, 10-year −14.38%.

## Round 1: investor

Students predict how each bond's price moves if rates rise one point (five ranges; scored
separately), then split $100,000 among cash, the 2-year, and the 10-year (whole percents that
add to 100; the server rejects anything else). At the reveal the move is drawn 50/50 (or set by
the instructor), and every portfolio is shown under **both** a rise and a fall, grouped by
10-year share, so the lesson is exposure rather than luck.

## Round 2: bank (SVB)

$100 of deposits (cost 2.5%) and $10 of equity: $110 split among cash (3.0%), the 2-year, and the
10-year. If rates hold, the bank earns a year's interest. If rates rise two points, 30% of
deposits are withdrawn, paid from cash, then 2-year bonds, then 10-year bonds sold at market
prices (realized losses). The bank **fails** if it cannot pay the withdrawals or if, at market
prices, its remaining assets are worth less than the deposits it still owes. The projector also
counts failed banks that still look solvent with bonds carried at cost, as held-to-maturity
accounting does. Example: everything in the 10-year → sells $30 at 85.6 (realized loss $5.04);
book equity $4.96, market equity −$5.80: fails. Demo (40 simulated banks, rates rose): 23 failed,
all 23 solvent at cost.

## Run (about 20 minutes)

Each round: Show round → Open choices → Close choices → Reveal. For round 2, consider setting
"What rates do" to **Rise** so the failure happens; the table shows both worlds either way.

**Debrief.** Why did the 10-year move four times as much as the 2-year? Who reached for yield in
round 2, and what did it earn if rates held? Why did the failed banks look fine at cost? How is
this SVB (08.6), and what would deposit insurance or a lender of last resort have changed
(Bank Run, Mar 4)?

**Fallback.** The bond pricing spreadsheet: three portfolios on the board, repriced under ±1 point.
