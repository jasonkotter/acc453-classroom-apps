# Defend the Peg

Apr 1, after 13.3 (pegs and crises). Bank Run's structure with a central bank defending a fixed
exchange rate. Runs on the shared toolkit.

## Rules

- Everyone holds 100 pesos, pegged at 1 peso = $1. The central bank's dollar reserves cover a set
  share of all pesos; an IMF credit line can add more.
- A share of holders (20%) are importers who must convert in window 1, assigned privately.
- In each window (3 by default) a holder may convert to dollars at the peg; before the first
  window ends, everyone also guesses what share of the class will have converted.
- The central bank pays each window in turn. If a window's requests exceed the dollars left, the
  peg breaks: that window's converters split what is left, later converters get nothing at the
  peg, and the peso falls by the devaluation (40%), so holders end with $60. If the peg holds,
  holders earn a $2 premium ($102). Doing nothing counts as holding.
- The instructor's browser counts each window and makes conversions final, as in Bank Run; the
  window open when the round closes is counted at the reveal.

## Treatments

1. **Thin reserves** (35%): the peg survives only if few beyond the importers run.
2. **Ample reserves** (90%): no plausible run breaks it.
3. **Thin reserves plus an IMF line** (35% + 50%): the backstop, as in Bank Run's lender of last resort.

Demo (40 simulated holders): the three presets held (8–9 conversions, mostly importers); a custom
round at 15% reserves broke in window 1, every holder ran, and the $600 of reserves went out at $15
a converter.
