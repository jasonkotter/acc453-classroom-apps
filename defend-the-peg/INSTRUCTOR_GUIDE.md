# Instructor guide: Defend the Peg

**Class:** Thursday, April 1, after 13.3 (pegs and crises). **Book:** Ch. 13. **Time:** about
15–20 minutes, before or instead of the Thailand 1997 case.

## Goal

This is Bank Run with a central bank in place of the bank. Everyone holds pesos pegged to the
dollar, and the central bank promises to convert them at the peg until its dollar reserves run
out. A peg can survive with thin reserves if few people convert, and fail with the same reserves
if many do. Students should leave able to explain why a currency crisis is a run, why the
expectation of devaluation can cause it, and why a credit line that is barely used can stop one.

## How a round works

1. **Everyone holds 100 pesos**, pegged at 1 peso = $1. The central bank's dollar reserves cover a
   set share of all pesos; an IMF credit line can add more.
2. **Importers.** 20% of holders are importers who must convert in window 1; they are told
   privately.
3. **Three windows.** In each, a holder may convert at the peg. Before the first window ends,
   everyone also guesses what share of the class will have converted.
4. **The central bank pays each window in turn.** If a window asks for more dollars than are
   left, the peg breaks: that window's converters split what is left, later converters get
   nothing at the peg, and the peso falls 40%, so holders end with $60. If the peg holds, holders
   earn a $2 premium and end with $102. Doing nothing counts as holding.

With 55 students and thin reserves (35%), the central bank has $1,925, enough for 19 conversions.
The 11 importers use 11 of them, so the peg breaks if 9 more holders convert.

## Rounds and timing

| Time | Round | What to say before opening |
|---|---|---|
| 2 min | Join and rules | Read the opening script. |
| 4 min | 1. Thin reserves (35% of pesos) | Nothing more. |
| 3 min | 2. Thin reserves again (optional) | "Same central bank. A different set of you are importers." |
| 3 min | 3. Thin reserves plus an IMF line (35% + 50%) | "The IMF has promised the central bank another 50% of all pesos in dollars if it needs them." |
| 3 min | 4. Ample reserves (90%) | Optional; describe it if time is short. |
| 4 min | Debrief | |

After each reveal the treatment menu moves to the next item in its own order (thin, ample, IMF),
so to replay thin reserves or to run the IMF round before ample, pick it in the menu before
**Show next round**. Each round: **Open conversions**, end each window, then **Reveal**. If the peg breaks before the
last window, the button changes to **End round: the peg has broken**. Give the first window about
30 seconds and later ones about 15.

## Setup

Create the session, open the projector screen on the classroom display, and wait for the joined
count to match the room.

## Opening script

"Each of you holds 100 pesos, and the central bank promises that a peso is worth a dollar. Hold
your pesos and you'll earn a little interest: $102 at the end. Convert and you get $100 now. Some
of you are importers and have to convert; your phone will tell you. The central bank has enough
dollars for the importers and some more. If it runs out, the peso is devalued by 40%."

## What to expect

This has not been run with students. In the demo and the live rehearsal, simulated holders rarely
converted beyond the importers, and the peg held in all three presets. A custom round with
reserves at 15% broke in window 1, and the stored payouts were exact: the converters split the
reserves, and the holders got $60. Students who have just played Bank Run on Mar 4 may run faster
than the bots, especially in a repeated thin-reserves round, and the IMF round should calm them.

## Debrief questions

**On screen.** **Begin debrief** puts three things on the projector and on every phone: an *explain* prompt that quotes this round's numbers, a *transfer* question, and a private exit check (one question on the phone; the reasoning and a common mistake appear after the student answers). Give pairs about two minutes for the first two, then a minute for the exit check. Exit-check answers are not recorded, graded, or timed. When you show the next round, the projector also lists what changed from the last round and what stayed fixed, and asks for a prediction before anyone plays. The questions below go deeper.

- Was the peg's fate decided by the reserves or by what everyone expected?
- Look at the guesses. Did the students who converted expect more conversions than those who held?
- Why did the IMF line matter even when it was barely used?
- Thailand, July 1997: what is the same as this game, and what is different?
- Compare Bank Run: what is different about a central bank's promise from a bank's?

## Fallback without the app

Bank Run's paper version with the labels changed: one in five slips marked as an importer, and a
show of hands for converting, with the reserve threshold written on the board.
